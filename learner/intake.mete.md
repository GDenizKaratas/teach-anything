# Mentor notes (intake)

Written by the mentor who set this repo up. Onboarding confirms it with the learner instead of asking everything again. Context only: what they actually know is measured from their answers.

## Who
Has lived in Australia for about 7 years and is building their own small business there, **NOA Matcha**: an online shop for matcha (tea, tools, gift sets) plus a "Matcha & Ceramic Painting Workshop" run in many Australian cities, and private group events (corporate team days, hens, birthdays). Mechatronics graduate.

## Goal
Two connected goals:
1. **Online marketing for their own business:** understand how customers find and choose a small business online, and be able to plan, run and judge their own marketing with their own judgment (website, social media, ads, email), using their real business as the running example.
2. **Economics as general literacy + personal investing:** how money really works (what money is, prices, inflation, interest rates, central banks, why the economy moves the way it does) and enough personal finance to make their own investment decisions sensibly (saving vs investing, risk and return, diversification, compounding, fees, common products). **Not academic level:** no formal models for their own sake.

Website: https://noamatcha.com/ (Shopify shop, workshop bookings per city, newsletter, Google reviews, Instagram/TikTok/Facebook/YouTube/Pinterest). Use what is publicly there as context, but ask the learner what actually brings customers and what they struggle with; the site doesn't tell you that.

## What matters most
Judgment over vocabulary: knowing *why* something works, how to measure it, and when a claim or a result is wrong (an ad platform's numbers, a "guaranteed" investment, an AI tool's marketing advice). AI tools can write ad copy and posts; the learner should be able to brief them well and judge what they produce.

Marketing examples should come from their own business (matcha, workshops, Australia). Economics examples stay general and everyday, and connect to the business only where it is natural (price, margin, cost of getting a customer). Investing is education, not personal financial advice: teach how to reason and what to ask, never recommend specific products.

## Starting point
No formal background in marketing or economics. But the business is already running (workshops in many cities, 100+ Google reviews, newsletter, several social channels), so they likely have hands-on marketing experience without the underlying principles. Don't treat them as a zero-knowledge beginner in marketing: probe what they already do and why, and build principles under their practice. Economics: measure from their answers. As a mechatronics graduate they are likely comfortable with numbers, systems and feedback loops; use that as a bridge where it truly fits, but don't assume it.

## Time
About 15 minutes a day as the target, but it varies. Keep lessons in small, complete chunks that fit 15 minutes; confirm in onboarding.

## Planned path (a proposal: new-track refines it with research; confirm with the learner)
Two tracks the learner moves between as their interest goes ("iç içe"), one active per session (`tools/learn use <track>`); review cards from both stay interleaved.
1. `online-marketing`: for their own business, from first principles (who the customer is, what they need, how they find you) to channels and measuring results. Australia-specific rules only where they apply to them (e.g. email/spam rules, consumer law on advertising claims).
2. `money-and-investing`: how money and the economy work, then personal investing in the Australian context (e.g. superannuation, tax basics only as far as needed; verify current facts with the researcher).

Bridge concepts (price, cost, margin, profit, cost of acquiring a customer) are taught once, in whichever track reaches them first, and referenced from the other. Switching tracks never skips a node's prerequisites: the dependency map of each track still holds. A side question from the other track is a value moment, answered briefly, then back to the active track.

## Language
Turkish explanations. Keep an English term next to it only when it is a standard, important term they will meet in practice (e.g. dönüşüm oranı (conversion rate), enflasyon (inflation), endeks fonu (index fund), ETF).
